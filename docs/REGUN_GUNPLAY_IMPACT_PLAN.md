# Regun Gunplay Impact Plan

**3 November 2026 · Design and implementation plan · 24 of 24 current weapons**

The goal is to make players feel a shot leave the weapon, recognize where it landed, and see what it did to the target. A hit on an unarmored mummy, a shield, a steel boss plate, and bare stone should give four different answers. Start from the current damage and timing. Treat any change to actual displacement or stagger as an explicit combat balance decision, with its own comparison and review.

## 1. What the current code tells us

- [The loadout enum](../lib/data/loadout.dart) contains 24 selectable weapons. [WeaponFeel](../lib/presentation/weapon_feel.dart) already distinguishes kick, recovery, muzzle flash, projectile silhouette and impact size. [Weapon art](../lib/components/weapons/weapon_art.dart) animates receiver parts, shell insertion, magazine swaps, a spinning revolver cylinder, and several power-cell motions. Start from those assets and sockets.
- [Projectile contact](../lib/components/weapons/bullet_component.dart) calculates the swept contact point and passes it to damage, but the ordinary impact sprite uses the target position. That weakens the connection between the projectile and the hit, especially on big bodies. Several special attack paths own separate effects, so changing the bullet path alone would leave a large part of the roster behind.
- [WeaponReaction](../lib/systems/weapon_reaction.dart) already specifies force, decay, and stagger for all 24. [CombatEnemy](../lib/components/enemies/combat_enemy.dart) gates displacement at .24 seconds and strong stagger at .65 seconds, with a stronger-hit override for force. Shields, elites and planted bodies resist differently. Crowds and walls can consume most of the visible travel. A held pose and a bright tint can confirm a hit without showing which way the force went.
- [CombatSystem](../lib/systems/combat_system.dart) is the accepted-damage path. Ordinary bullets, special weapons, support, secondary effects, and some boss damage paths reach it with different context. Guard and armor rejection must have their own contact cue, and a rejected hit must never look like flesh damage.
- [Reload state](../lib/notifiers/game_notifier.dart) owns actual completion and cue timing. The current enum enables tactical reload support across the roster, which differs from an older design note about Carbine only. Use the effective runtime and checkpoint behavior as baseline. Shotgun shell insertion, Fast Reload, survivor modifiers, and quick refill each need a readable path. Preserve the accepted shotgun Lottie.
- [Online presentation](../lib/widgets/online_arena.dart) already tracks shot timestamps and draws weapon recoil. Local and authoritative contact feedback need different triggers. The current hosted roster is capability dependent. Use the actual negotiated weapon set when verifying it.
- Archived [gameplay footage](validation/upgrade-impact-release-2026-10-26/breach-full-heavyLoad.mp4) shows a readable muzzle flash and white hit frame. It is an older upgrade review with an overlapping pulse. Use it as an example of how easily a bright cue can dominate a smaller contact cue. Capture a clean baseline from the current build for each weapon before comparing.

## 2. A shared vocabulary for all weapons

| Beat | What the player should read | Presentation direction |
|---|---|---|
| Windup | What is about to happen and when | Reserve a pronounced windup for charge, spin, or committed releases already present in gameplay. Show readiness through a mechanical seat, glow, or short sound. |
| Discharge | What left the gun | Align recoil, sound attack and flash on the accepted release. Put motion in the receiver, hands and silhouette. Match the firing mechanism. |
| Flight | Where the shot is going | Preserve recognizable pellet, slug, rocket, ring, beam or ground geometry. Keep flight effects subordinate to hostile warnings. |
| Contact | Where it struck and what it struck | Draw at the swept surface or the authoritative overlap. Match debris and sound to tissue, chitin, shield, armor, terrain, or energy. |
| Reaction | How the target absorbed the blow | Use a quick directional body snap, a short hold, then recovery. Add visible footwork or actual shove where the enemy and collision permit it. |
| Recovery | When the gun can fire again | Show the pump, bolt, cylinder or chamber settling at its existing cue. Preserve responsiveness and the ready state during movement. |

Give each weapon one dominant beat. For example, the Rivet Driver builds anticipation at the stuck rivet and earns its strongest cue on reload detonation. The Carbine stays light per bullet and gets character from a consistent receiver rhythm. Keep these differences perceptible with the camera steady and the sound muted.

Use warm ivory and brass for ballistic discharge, chipped iron for armor, restrained organic fragments for bodies, and established ice and electrical materials for those weapons. Texture clusters, occlusion and direction should communicate substance. Avoid a single full-body white flash as the sole sign of contact. Preserve boss section masks, ambient grading and real collision boundaries. Use a compact edge flick and sound on planted targets.

## 4. Proposed code changes

These are implementation targets for a subsequent task. Start by recording the currently installed behavior and log the effective loadout revision with each capture. Work from runtime data, since older review documents contain superseded values.

1. **Carry reliable contact information.** In [BulletComponent](../lib/components/weapons/bullet_component.dart), emit feedback at the swept contact point and carry direction, weapon, release or projectile ID, target class and accepted/blocked outcome. Use the real solid intersection for wall effects. In [CombatSystem](../lib/systems/combat_system.dart), distinguish accepted damage, armor/guard absorption and death. Avoid adding a second damage path. Put deduplication after knowing which feedback occurred, so the first small pellet cannot suppress a stronger contact in the same volley.
2. **Cover every emission path.** Audit [ArsenalSystem](../lib/systems/arsenal_system.dart), [EndgameArsenal](../lib/systems/endgame_arsenal.dart), and [FrontierArsenal](../lib/systems/frontier_arsenal.dart) for contact, detonation, pulse, continuing contact and return. Wire their distinct events through shared rules for material, cue priority and budget while preserving specialized artwork and attack shape. Treat a charged rail shot, a sustained beam, and a returning cutter as different event lifecycles. No artificial bullet or fake damage event to drive effects.
3. **Separate physical motion from visual reaction.** Keep [WeaponReaction](../lib/systems/weapon_reaction.dart) and collision checks authoritative. In [CombatEnemy](../lib/components/enemies/combat_enemy.dart) and the enemy body renderers, add a bounded direction-aware pose overlay after meaningful contact. Stop the visual offset at the silhouette scale, keep the feet/contact shadow aligned with physical movement, and retain planted or authored boss poses. Read actual movement before tuning force. Record peak and net displacement, stun uptime, interrupted moves and the effect of wall/crowd constraints. Increase physical force only when the phone comparison justifies the change.
4. **Rework the contact artwork.** In [WeaponImpact](../lib/components/weapons/weapon_impact.dart), use the contact point, direction, material and event tier. Put the most legible cluster on the first frame. Fade a small secondary fragment or localized dust behind it. Keep boss section impacts local and apply the existing ambient palette. Preserve caps and avoid full-canvas compositing. Use existing material textures where they read well. Add an asset only when it solves a specific readability problem.
5. **Give the camera a limited role.** Add a short render-only translation driven by a small number of ranked impulses. Use an initial comparison around 1–3 logical phone pixels for heavy discharge and a fast decay around 70–120 ms. These are starting values. Cap overlap with boss camera effects, preserve camera bounds and keep input, crosshair logic, HUD and touch targets stable. No repeated shake per pellet, beam tick, chain jump, online duplicate or sustained automatic bullet. Reduced Motion removes camera translation and retains local cues. Test camera-on and camera-off versions separately.
6. **Make each reload legible without retiming it.** Keep [GameNotifier](../lib/notifiers/game_notifier.dart) as the clock and cue owner. Drive a small set of named pose beats in [WeaponArt](../lib/components/weapons/weapon_art.dart): disengage, expose or eject, seat, lock, ready. Each weapon chooses its gestures from the table. Fit both tactical and empty timing, including modifiers and old saves. A Revolver roll is a presentation turn around its grip. Firing direction, ammo, and the time controls return stay authoritative. Keep mirrored controls, cover visibility and the accepted shotgun Lottie.
7. **Mix the action and the material separately.** Review the [SoLoud routing](../lib/utils/soloud_play.dart) and existing recorded weapon sources before generating more audio. Match the contact transient to the material. Compare one dominant sample for a release or volley plus a quiet contact variant, with clear limits on crowd and sustained noise. Use audible mids and a short tail that reads on phone speakers. Protect enemy warnings, low ammo and reload-ready cues. Consider a single haptic on selected heavy moments under the existing Haptics preference after sound and animation read well.
8. **Keep multiplayer cues tied to authority.** Use existing shot and contact event IDs and negotiated capability. Immediate local muzzle response can be predicted. Enemy damage, armor breaks and kills use confirmed events. Reconcile predicted and confirmed release IDs, and expire stale feedback across reconnects. Verify per-weapon admission in the matching server build. Apply the same effect rules to Solo and Co-op wherever the weapon is admitted.

## 5. Capture and review workflow

Use the current release as the baseline. Record phone-scale frames in the actual gameplay camera with HUD and controls on. Save full-frame real-time clips with sound and frame strips around the muzzle, contact and reload. Zoomed crops help diagnose alignment and animation, but phone playback decides readability. Record device, build, balance revision, distance, target type, weapon state, and settings with each clip.

| Pass | Scope | Review |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | All 24. A miss, a clean hit, and an empty reload per weapon. Add a partial manual reload wherever supported. | Establish what already reads, where contact actually lands, and what current phone performance looks like. |
| Shared rules | Shotgun, Revolver, Carbine, Rail Lance, Cinder Hose, and Ring Caster as representative checks. | Isolate contact and target reaction first, then compare with camera and audio accents. The slice proves the rules used by the full roster. |
| Full roster | Cover all 24 rows. Test each unique payoff: freeze, pierce, detonation, return, combo, splash, pull, echo, chain or capture. | No weapon is complete until it passes its own phone check. Use the same target and conditions for comparisons. |
| Stress and accessibility | Mixed crowd, rapid fire and Scatter upgrades, busy hazard floor, boss armor, UI edges, low volume, sound off, Reduced Motion, and permitted Co-op. | Attacks and exits stay visible. No excessive movement or strobing, repeated confirmations, audio clipping, new combat hitch or reduced control. |
| Gameplay regression | Confirm damage, ammo, fire and reload clocks, sweep and ricochet, boss rules, pause, Continue and restart. Where actual force changes, verify enemy counterplay. | Prove measured behavior with focused tests and playtest the final signed candidate on the reference iPhone. |

Use a blinded A/B when possible. Ask which shot felt heavier, where it hit, and whether the player could keep aiming and read the next threat. Then have them repeat the action for a full encounter. Test source audio and final mix on the phone, and compare camera-on and camera-off on the same clip. A brief impact that looks good in a still must also feel good in motion.

**Completion evidence:** one reviewed row and capture set per weapon, verified outcomes for body/armor/cover/kill, a comfort and performance review on the reference phone, and matching admitted Co-op behavior. Record any tune that changes physical knockback or stagger with its before/after displacement and counterplay results.
